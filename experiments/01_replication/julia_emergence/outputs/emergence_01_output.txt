=== I. f = cross(f): the paradox that ticks ===
  start ·  →  cycle: · → ▮  (period 2)
  start ▮  →  cycle: ▮ → ·  (period 2)
  Statically: contradiction. Dynamically: an oscillator.
  Neither marked nor unmarked — a value that IS its own
  alternation. Spencer-Brown called this imaginary, as √-1
  is to the reals: not in the old space, orthogonal to it.

=== II. mutual re-entry: memory emerges, uninvited ===
  press SET    → held state: ·▮
  release all  → held state: ·▮   (it REMEMBERS)
  press RESET  → held state: ▮·
  release all  → held state: ▮·   (it REMEMBERS)
  Persistence — the first candidate for 'thing' — is not
  a substance. It is a loop that keeps re-entering itself.

=== III. an ecology of self-reference (n=12, 60 worlds) ===
  6 worlds settled into fixed points  — 'things', frozen re-entry
  48 worlds settled into period 2      — 'clocks', bare oscillation
  5 worlds settled into period 4      — longer rhythms, proto-process
  1 worlds settled into period 10      — longer rhythms, proto-process
  NOR is logically universal: this fauna is not decorative.
  Given size, such worlds can compute anything computable —
  therefore predicting their settling is, in general,
  equivalent to the halting problem. We must LIVE the
  dynamics to know it. No view from outside time is sold here.

=== IV. residue ===
• Time appeared as the resolution of a paradox we chose
  not to forbid. Different clock choices (Choice 1) may
  dissolve or breed different attractors. Unchecked here.
• 'Things' arrived as fixed points, 'memory' as bistable
  loops, 'process' as cycles — all from one move: letting
  a distinction re-enter its own form.
• This ledger is itself a re-entrant form: a text that
  describes the system that includes the text. Its fixed
  point, if any, is not computable from inside. Stop here,
  at the edge we can see, knowing there are edges we cannot.
