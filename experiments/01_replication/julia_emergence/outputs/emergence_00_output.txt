=== From one distinction, form ===
void                     -> marked? false
cross()                  -> marked? true
cross(cross())           -> marked? false
cross() cross()          -> marked? true

=== Boolean logic, uninvited, arrives ===
F AND F = false   |   F OR F = false
F AND T = false   |   F OR T = true
T AND F = false   |   T OR F = true
T AND T = true   |   T OR T = true
NOT T = false,  NOT F = true

=== Number, born from refusing a law ===
three marks, uncondensed: 3  (condensed they were merely: marked = true)

=== Gödel/Turing residue ===
This program cannot prove: (1) its ledger is complete,
(2) its host is consistent, (3) its own halting, in general.
The unmarked state is not IN the calculus of marks.
It is what the calculus is written on. We stop here,
at the edge we can see, knowing there are edges we cannot.
